## Why I built it

I wanted to ride my exercise bike, look up at the TV, and just tell my computer what to do, then watch it happen.

So at first there was no key to press. While {NAME} ran, it listened. That lasted until it overheard me
talking *about* {NAME} ("the voice doesn't feel like a real conversation") and the agent set out to send a
Slack message to someone it had made up. I hit pause about ten seconds in.

Now you talk to it like a walkie-talkie. Hold the fn key, say what you want, let go. While the key is up the
microphone is off and nothing goes anywhere. For the bike, tap fn once instead: that opens a hands-free
conversation, and it closes by itself after 20 seconds of quiet. If you really do want it listening all the
time, that is still there as an option.

## What it is

{art({
	name: 'demo', wide: true,
	alt: "Animated demo: a spoken request appears as a caption, a coral ring and a ghost cursor move across two apps on a Mac desktop, an approval card pops up and is approved by voice, and the task finishes.",
})}

{NAME} is a free, open-source app for your Mac. You talk, and an AI does the clicking and typing
in the apps you already use. The AI is the coding agent you probably already have: Claude Code or Codex.

The point is that it works where you can see it. Not in a hidden browser in the cloud. Not in a
background window you never see. Right in front of you, where you can say "stop".

Yesterday I said "find three sushi places near the Ferry Building and put them in a new note",
and took my hands off the keyboard. Maps opened. A search ran. A note appeared with three names
in it. I watched every step.

It talks while it works, too. Say "open Maps" and you hear "OK, opening Maps" right away, then news as
it happens. That wasn't true at first. The first version was a terse butler: after "open Maps" it went
quiet for about fifteen seconds, then said "I've opened Maps". It did not feel like talking to a person,
so I rebuilt the voice around one idea: it owes you a reply to everything you say, and it isn't done until
you've actually heard one. If a reply gets lost, it says it again later.

## Three things that make it different

### You see every step

{art({
	name: 'feature-see',
	alt: "A coral ring and a ghost cursor land on a button a moment before it is clicked, with a caption naming the step.",
})}

Before {NAME} touches anything, a coral ring and a ghost cursor land on the exact spot. They
arrive a beat early, so your eye gets there first.

A caption says what the step is. Another shows what it heard you say, so if it misheard, you know
at once.

### You say yes out loud

{art({
	name: 'feature-say',
	alt: "An approval card on screen, and a speech bubble saying yes, I approve.",
})}

Anything risky stops and asks. A card pops up with a soft chime. Click it, or say "yes, I approve":
with fn held, or from across the room while a conversation is open.

It is picky about that yes. "Ok" doesn't count. "Maybe" doesn't count. A mumble doesn't count. The
voice decides what it heard, and then a second, separate check in code has to agree. Of 169 test cases
that must not count as a yes, none got through.

{NAME} doesn't take "ok" for an answer.

### It steps aside

{art({
	name: 'feature-aside',
	alt: "A person typing in one window while the ring waits politely at the edge of the screen.",
})}

You and {NAME} share one screen, one cursor and one keyboard. So while you type, it backs off. It
never grabs the window you're working in.

When you pause, or say "go ahead", it carries on. It feels like sharing a computer, not lending
one out.

This is Hands, a small coral seal. It's called Hands because it doesn't have any. So "Look ma, no hands" is less a slogan than a

It balances the coral ring on its nose. Wherever the ring lands is the thing it is about to

It loves an audience and has strict manners. Its one rule: **no approval, no seal.**

## Try it

You need a Mac on macOS 15 or later, Node 20 or later, and Claude Code or Codex, logged in.
`lookma doctor` checks for everything else.

```sh
git clone https://github.com/liuyoshio/lookma
cd lookma
npm install && npm link
# check that everything it needs is in place
lookma doctor
# start it, with the folders it may work in
lookma ~/Projects
```

Add `--agent codex` to let Codex do the work instead of Claude Code. Then hold fn, say "turn on dark mode", and let go.

One setup step catches people: fn is also the Globe key, and a tap of it may switch your input source or
open the emoji picker. Set System Settings > Keyboard > "Press Globe key to" to Do Nothing. Control-Space
still switches input sources. `lookma doctor` checks this for you.

Voice needs an OpenAI key: copy `.env.example` to `.env` and set `OPENAI_API_KEY`. Typing works
without one. It is MIT licensed.

Then two small asks. <a href={REPO_URL}>Star the repo</a> if you like the idea. And record Hands
doing one small chore on your Mac, and post the clip with 🦭🙌. The best demos of this thing won't
be mine.

## What it can't do yet

This is early, and I'd rather you hear it from me.

- Mac only, and only your main display.
- It can right-click and drag now, but a drag stays inside one app.
- It's not fast. A task with a few steps still takes several seconds. Each click or keystroke takes from well under a second to about a second and a half; most of the wait is the agent thinking between steps.
- It sometimes says it will do something and then doesn't start it. I'm watching that before I pick a fix.
- It runs on your Mac, with no account and no tracking. The voice needs an OpenAI key, and what you say while you hold fn (or in a conversation) goes to OpenAI. Typing works without a key.
- It can be wrong, and it is using your real computer. Start with small things.

Watch it on anything that matters. Watching is the whole idea.

## What's next

Next come more voices: Gemini Live, then an offline voice that never leaves your Mac. After that, a "Hey
Lookma" wake word, as an option next to the fn key. The agent and the voice are both plug-ins, one folder each, so a new one
shouldn't need anything else to change.
